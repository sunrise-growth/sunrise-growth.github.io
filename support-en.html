Wenjiao問筊

Support & FAQ

Last updated: November 28, 2026

Wenjiao (問筊) is a simulation and hands-on experience of Taiwanese temple culture: moon blocks, worship, poem lots, villain hitting, and the lunar calendar and solar terms every day. This page answers common questions. If you can’t find what you need, write to us.

The app’s interface is in Traditional Chinese. Below, screen and button names are given in Chinese as they appear in the app, followed by an English translation.

Getting started

What do I need to use it?

An iPhone with iOS 26 or later. The interface is in Traditional Chinese. You don’t need an account, and the app works offline; you only need an internet connection to buy a membership or support the team.

Does it cost anything?

Moon blocks, poem lots (the poem, its meaning and the plain-language reading), worship, villain hitting, the calendar and reminders are all free. In poem lot results, the full readings for aspects such as career, love and health are member content; without a subscription you can still read the opening of each one. Membership comes in monthly and six-month plans, and the prices and offers shown in the app are what apply.

The box on the altar table in the worship tab lets you support the team (支持開發團隊) with an optional in-app purchase, which doesn’t give you anything in return. The membership plans and the support screen only open when you tap them yourself.

Do I have to answer the temple keeper’s questions the first time?

No. The first time you open the app, the screen says 有什麼心事嗎？ (Is something on your mind?). Tap 擲筊 (Moon blocks) and the temple keeper asks for your name, gender, birthday and birth hour. You can tap 先不說 (Rather not say) on each question, or tap 略過 (Skip) at the top right to skip them all. If you do answer, the details appear on your name card, in the greeting on the calendar tab, and in the sample wording for stating your request. You can change or clear them later in 設定 › 個人資料 (Settings › Personal details).

What tabs does the app have?

There are four tabs at the bottom of the screen: 日曆 (Calendar), 參拜 (Worship), 擲筊 (Moon blocks) and 打小人 (Villain hitting). Poem lots don’t have their own tab; you reach them from the lot cylinder on the altar table in the worship tab.

Where are the settings?

Tap the gear at the top right of any tab.

What’s in Settings?
  • The name card at the top: your personal details.
  • 請示紀錄 (History) and 擲筊小冊 (Moon block booklet).
  • 畫面 (Display): appearance, text size, and hints.
  • 擲筊與抽籤 (Moon blocks & poem lots): how you ask, how you draw lots, and shake to throw and draw.
  • 聲音與提醒 (Sound & reminders): sound effects, background sound, haptics; daily quote and solar term reminders, plus reminders for deity birthdays, Tianshe Day, the 1st and 15th, festivals and Zuoya.
  • 桌面小工具 (Widgets): steps for adding widgets, with a preview of each one.
  • 資料與隱私 (Data & privacy): clear all records, privacy policy, disclaimer, contact us, and the support website.

At the very bottom you’ll see the app version.

Why is there a moon block animation every time I open the app?

That’s the opening. It only plays when the app starts fresh, not when you come back to it from the background, and you can tap the screen to skip it. It’s just a short performance, not a throw on your behalf: nothing is recorded, and it has nothing to do with the results of any later throw.

Moon blocks

How do I throw the moon blocks?
  1. Tap the 擲筊 (Moon blocks) tab at the bottom, choose a deity, and tap 開始請示 (Start).
  2. For your first three times, a card first shows how to throw; tap 我知道了 (Got it) when you’ve read it. The first time you reach the throwing screen, a reminder asks you to state your request silently; tap 說好了 (Done) when you have.
  3. On the throwing screen, any of these three ways works:
    • Tap anywhere on the screen.
    • Swipe up. The faster you swipe, the higher the blocks fly.
    • Hold your phone firmly and shake it. You don’t need to throw your phone, so please don’t.
  4. Tap again while the blocks are still in the air to jump straight to the result.

Once the result appears, you can throw again (再擲一次), note down what you asked, share a card, or tap 完成 (Done).

What’s the difference between standard and careful asking?

With standard asking (一般請示), one throw gives you a result, and you can throw again. With careful asking (慎重請示), you need three shengbei in a row for it to count; if a xiaobei, yinbei or libei comes up along the way, that round of asking ends.

Switch between them in 設定 › 擲筊與抽籤 › 請示方式 (Settings › Moon blocks & poem lots › How you ask).

Shaking the phone doesn’t work, or I’d rather not shake it.

Hold the phone firmly and give it a few shakes in a row; a gentle wobble won’t throw the blocks.

If you’d rather not shake it, go to 設定 › 擲筊與抽籤 (Settings › Moon blocks & poem lots) and turn off 搖一搖手機來擲筊、抽籤 (Shake to throw and draw). Once it’s off, shaking does nothing, and you can still tap.

I have a question about how it works.

The temple keeper sits at the top of the 擲筊 (Moon blocks) tab. Tap him and choose from nine common questions. For a fuller guide, see 設定 › 擲筊小冊 (Settings › Moon block booklet).

Which deities are there, and which one should I choose?

There are twelve: Mazu (媽祖), Guan Gong (關公), Guanyin (觀音), Yue Lao (月老), Tudigong (土地公), Wenchang (文昌), Tiangong (天公), Santaizi (三太子), Caishenye (財神爺), Jigong (濟公), Taisui (太歲) and Huye (虎爺). Each deity’s card lists what people commonly ask about, and tapping it shows a short introduction. If you can’t decide, choose the one you usually pray to.

What are shengbei, xiaobei, yinbei and libei?

They depend on which side of each block lands facing up:

  • Shengbei (聖杯): one flat side and one round side up. Folk tradition reads it as “yes.”
  • Xiaobei (笑杯): both flat sides up. Folk tradition likens it to a smile without an answer; try putting your question more clearly and ask again.
  • Yinbei (陰杯): both round sides up. Folk tradition reads it as “not a good idea”; try a different approach, or ask another day.
  • Libei (立杯): a block lands standing on its edge. It’s extremely rare, and folk readings of it vary.

These are all traditional folk readings. To see how results are produced, read Are the results accurate?

What if a block lands standing up?

A standing block is rare. The result is shown and recorded as usual; the statistics in 設定 › 請示紀錄 (Settings › History) count standing blocks separately. The folk custom is not to jump to conclusions: settle your mind, state your request again, and throw once more. In careful asking, a standing block ends that round.

How should I state my request? Do I need to write it down?

Silently tell the deity your name, date of birth and where you live, then say what you’d like to ask. You don’t need to write it down, and it isn’t recorded. For example:

“I am [name], born on [date], living in [district, city]. Today I sincerely ask: [your question]. Is this something I should go ahead with?”

Try to ask something that can be answered with “yes” or “no,” and ask about one thing at a time. If you’ve filled in your personal details, the sample wording includes your name and birthday.

Do I need to light incense before throwing the moon blocks?

At a temple, people usually light incense first and then state their request to the deity. In the app, you don’t need incense to throw: once you’ve stated your request silently, go ahead and throw. The card on how to throw only appears for your first three times, and the reminder to state your request only the first time. If you’d like to light incense, go to the 參拜 (Worship) tab, place offerings, light incense and put your palms together.

How do I note down what I asked, and add what happened later?

When the result appears, tap 記下問題 (Note question) to write down what you just asked (optional). Later, open that entry in 設定 › 請示紀錄 (Settings › History) to edit the question, or write what happened afterwards under 事後回顧 (Looking back). This text is stored only on your phone.

How do I share a result? Will my question be shared too?

When the result appears, tap 分享 (Share) to see a preview of the card, then tap 分享卡片 (Share card). If you noted down a question, you decide whether to turn on 附上記下的問題 (Include my question); it’s off by default. To save the image to your photo library, choose 儲存影像 (Save Image) in the share menu. Every entry in 設定 › 請示紀錄 (Settings › History) can be shared again too.

Worship

How do I place offerings and worship?
  1. Tap the 參拜 (Worship) tab. Below the altar table is a row of deities; tap one to switch to it, or swipe left or right on the altar table.
  2. Tap 開始參拜 (Start worship), then 選供品 (Choose offerings). Offerings come in nine categories: treasures (珍品), fruit, cakes, sweets, candy and snacks, drinks, savory snacks, flowers, and joss paper. Tap an item to add one; when you’re done, tap 兌換 (Exchange). If you go back without tapping it, nothing you picked on that page is kept.
  3. Your offerings wait in the tray at the bottom. Drag them one by one to wherever you’d like them on the altar table.
  4. Tap 上香 (Incense) and place the incense in the burner.
  5. Press and hold the screen with your palms together until the progress fills up.

If you’d rather skip the offerings, tap 直接上香 (Incense only).

To take an offering off the table, tap it, then tap the × that appears. Removing something you chose this time, before finishing your worship, gives you back its devotion points; removing something already offered does not.

The ? at the top of the worship tab is 怎麼參拜？ (How to worship). It walks you through the steps and explains how an altar table is usually arranged.

I’m not sure which joss paper to offer.

When choosing offerings, tap the temple keeper’s picture to the right of 兌換 (Exchange) to ask the keeper: how to choose joss paper, what kinds there are, who the deity is, which offerings are common, and whether joss paper has to be burned. Ask 金紙要怎麼選？ (How do I choose joss paper?) and the keeper tells you which joss paper is commonly used for the deity you’re worshipping and how much devotion it takes, then offers to choose for you: tap 幫我選好 (Choose for me) to add one of each suggested paper. If you don’t have enough devotion, the keeper tells you how much more you need and adds nothing. These are common folk practices; customs vary from temple to temple, so follow the temple’s own practice.

Some offerings show a 秘 (secret) mark and the name ？？？.

These are offerings you can’t get yet. Some become available when you reach a certain practice rank, and some only on certain days, such as the Lantern Festival or a deity’s birthday. The condition is written underneath, for example 升到弟子 (Reach 弟子) or 元宵當天 (On the Lantern Festival); once you meet it, the offering’s picture and name appear.

Can I move offerings once they’re on the table?

Yes. While you are arranging offerings, every offering on the table can be dragged to a new spot; anything you placed this time can be dragged down off the table to put it back in the tray. If you’d rather not drag, tap an offering in the tray and it goes to an empty spot on the table. When the table is full, you’ll see 供桌擺滿了 (The altar table is full); take something off to make room.

Does each deity have a separate altar table? How long do offerings stay?

Each deity has an altar table of their own. What you place today stays on that deity’s table, and the next day starts with a fresh table; dates follow Taiwan time. If you leave after choosing offerings but before lighting incense and putting your palms together, the offerings stay on the table so you can carry on next time.

Once you’ve worshipped a deity today, or your offerings are placed but the incense isn’t lit yet, 開始參拜 (Start worship) changes to 上香 (Incense), with 再添供品 (Add offerings) next to it; when the table is full, only 上香 (Incense) is shown.

How do I earn devotion points? Can I buy them?

No, you can’t buy them. Devotion points only come from:

  • 2,000 as a welcome gift the first time you use the app.
  • Lighting incense: 30 points each time you finish worshipping (lighting incense and putting your palms together), even without offerings. The worship tab also says 上香拜完一次，虔誠值+30 (+30 devotion points each time you finish worshipping) under 開始參拜 (Start worship).
  • Striking the wooden fish: 1 point per strike.
  • Meditating: 100 points for every full minute. You can choose from 3 minutes up to an hour; anything under a minute doesn’t count.
  • A bonus each time you reach a new practice rank.

Devotion points are exchanged for offerings; incense doesn’t cost any points. Offerings are a gesture of sincerity and don’t affect the results of moon blocks or poem lots.

Where are the wooden fish and meditation?

There are two small cards below the altar table in the worship tab: 敲木魚 (Wooden fish) and 打坐 (Meditate).

  • Wooden fish: each tap is one strike. Strike it one beat at a time; strikes that come too fast don’t count.
  • Meditate: choose how long you’d like to sit, then breathe in and out with the ring of light on screen. The screen won’t dim on its own while you sit.

What if I want to stop partway through meditating, or a call comes in?

If you switch to another app, lock the screen or get a call, meditation pauses. When you come back, you choose 繼續 (Continue) or 結束 (End). If you end early, the full minutes you’ve already sat still count; any part of a minute does not.

What is the practice rank?

Every devotion point you earn from the wooden fish, meditation and lighting incense adds the same amount to your practice; points you spend on offerings never reduce it. When your practice reaches the next threshold, you go up a rank. There are nine ranks: 善男信女, 香客, 信士 (信女), 弟子, 爐下弟子, 師兄 (師姐), 大德, 頭家 and 爐主.

Each new rank comes with a gift of devotion points, plus more offerings and more decorations or styles for the altar table. Tap the seal at the top left of the worship tab to open 參拜足跡 (Worship journal), where you can see your rank, choose decorations, and check your worship streak and offerings collection. Your rank doesn’t affect the results of moon blocks or poem lots.

How are the worship streak and the offerings collection counted?
  • Streak: a day counts once you’ve finished lighting incense and putting your palms together; placing offerings alone doesn’t count. If you haven’t worshipped yet today but did yesterday, your streak is still going; skip a whole day and it starts over. Dates follow Taiwan time. 參拜足跡 (Worship journal) shows your current streak, your longest streak, and your total number of visits.
  • Offerings collection: each offering you have completed a worship with lights up and shows how many times you’ve offered it. Ones you haven’t worshipped with yet are black silhouettes named ？？？, and appear once you light incense and put your palms together with them on the table; placing them on the table without finishing the worship doesn’t count.

Poem lots

How do I draw a poem lot?

By default, the app follows temple practice: you throw the moon blocks to ask first, and throw again to confirm the lot you drew.

  1. Tap the 參拜 (Worship) tab and switch to the deity you’d like to draw from.
  2. Tap the lot cylinder at the back left of the altar table.
  3. Ask whether the deity is willing to give you a lot: tap the screen to throw the moon blocks, and when you get a shengbei, tap 開始抽籤 (Start drawing). The first time, a reminder asks you to state your request silently first; tap 說好了 (Done) when you have.
  4. Tap 搖籤筒 (Shake the cylinder) (or tap the cylinder, or shake your phone). Once a lot comes out, tap 擲筊確認 (Confirm with moon blocks).
  5. Throw again to ask whether this is the right lot; when you get a shengbei, tap 看籤詩 (Read the poem).

The poem page shows the poem in vertical lines with its grade, followed by a plain-language summary, a few reminders and notes on specific areas of life. At the bottom you can note down your question (記下問題) or share (分享).

If you’d rather draw and read straight away without throwing, switch to 直接抽 (Draw directly); see the next question.

Which set of lots does each deity use?
  • Guanyin: Guanyin 100 lots (觀音一百籤)
  • Guan Gong: Guandi 100 lots (關帝一百籤)
  • Yue Lao: Yue Lao lots (月老聖籤)
  • Mazu, Tudigong, Wenchang, Tiangong, Santaizi, Caishenye, Jigong, Taisui and Huye: Sixty Jiazi lots (六十甲子籤)

Can I draw a lot directly, without throwing moon blocks?

Yes. Choose an option in 設定 › 擲筊與抽籤 › 抽籤方式 (Settings › Moon blocks & poem lots › How you draw):

  • 擲筊確認 (Confirm with moon blocks, the default): the temple way. Throw the blocks to ask first, then confirm the lot you drew with one shengbei.
  • 直接抽 (Draw directly): shake the cylinder, and whichever lot comes out is yours.
  • 三聖杯確認 (Confirm with three shengbei): after drawing, you need three shengbei in a row for the lot to count, so you may need to draw a few times.

What if I keep failing to get a shengbei when confirming?
  • If you haven’t got a shengbei yet when asking whether the deity is willing to give you a lot: state your request a little more clearly in your mind, and tap 再擲一次 (Throw again).
  • If you don’t get a shengbei when asking whether this is the right lot: put the lot back in the cylinder and tap 重新抽籤 (Draw again). With 三聖杯確認 (Confirm with three shengbei) you need three in a row, so you may need to draw a few times.
  • If you go five throws in a row without a shengbei, the temple keeper comes to check on you. You can ask again, or tap 改天再來 (Come back another day) to end this draw.

Which areas do the notes cover?

The Sixty Jiazi, Guanyin and Guandi lots cover career, money, love, health, studies and travel. The Yue Lao lots cover being single, crushes, dating, marriage and getting back together. Some lots also include 版本差異 (Version differences), which explains how versions differ from place to place.

The notes are only general pointers. For health and other important decisions, please rely on a doctor or other professionals.

The poem is different from the one I saw at a temple.

Within the same set of lots, wording, order and interpretation vary from temple to temple. The original text in the app follows the versions commonly used in Taiwan and has been checked against other versions; the plain-language explanations are one reading that we wrote.

The grades for the Guanyin 100 and Guandi 100 lots are the ones those sets commonly carry. The Sixty Jiazi and Yue Lao lots traditionally have no printed grades; the 上上, 上籤, 中籤 and 下籤 grades on those slips are our own summary of each poem’s meaning, not a temple’s marking.

Can I turn off the animation when drawing a lot?

Drawing a lot plays a full-screen animation, with a more elaborate version for the highest-graded lots. It’s only an animation: which lot you get is decided before it starts.

When Reduce Motion is on in your iPhone settings (設定 › 輔助使用 › 動作), the full-screen animation is skipped and a simple cylinder-shaking animation plays instead.

Where are my poem lot records?

They’re kept with your moon block records in 設定 › 請示紀錄 (Settings › History). Each entry is titled with the lot you drew, with 向○○求籤 (Drew a lot from [deity]) underneath. Open it to read the poem and explanation again, see the moon block throws you made to confirm it, or share it.

Villain hitting

How does villain hitting work?

Villain hitting (打小人) is a folk custom found in Hong Kong and Taiwan, usually around the solar term Jingzhe (驚蟄) in early spring: you beat a paper figure with a shoe to let off pent-up frustration, then burn the paper.

  1. Tap the 打小人 (Villain hitting) tab. Use the row at the bottom to switch villains; there are five designs.
  2. Tap 開始打小人 (Start).
  3. Choose an item at the bottom, then tap the villain to hit him. You can also use your hands: swipe sideways across him quickly, press and hold him, grab him, drag him away and let go hard, pinch him with two fingers, or shake your phone.
  4. When you’ve had enough, tap 送走小人 (Send him off), or drag him straight into the brazier.

Which villains and items are there?

Five villains: 囂張仔 (the Show-off), 慣老闆 (the Bad Boss), 大嘴巴 (the Big Mouth), 白目仔 (the Clueless Guy) and 花心蘿蔔 (the Player). There are thirty items, from slippers, wooden clogs, a fly swatter and a frying pan to eggs, tomatoes and water balloons you can throw, plus a giant foot and a white tiger. The items sit in two rows that you can swipe through.

On the day of Jingzhe, the villain hitting tab says 今天驚蟄，習俗上是打小人的日子 (Today is Jingzhe, the traditional day for villain hitting); you can play on any other day too.

Is the name I write on a villain saved?

No. Tap 取名字 (Name him) to write a nickname of up to eight characters on the villain’s chest. It only lasts for that round, is cleared once you send him off, is never saved, and can’t be shared. The only thing the app records is how many villains you’ve sent off this year.

You’re hitting paper, and tradition avoids naming real people, so please use a nickname rather than someone’s real name.

What are tags, and how are they different from the name on the chest?

The name on the chest is a nickname you choose; if you don’t write one, the villain keeps his original label. Tags are the short words in a row under the villain, such as 長舌 (gossip), 搶功 (credit-stealer), 豬隊友 (useless teammate) and 奧客 (nightmare customer). You can pick several, and they’re only labels. Both the name and the tags last for that round only and are cleared once you send him off.

The villain says “Go on, hit me with a slipper!”

Sometimes the villain taunts you and names an item, which then lights up in the item rows; if he names a hand move, the screen shows you how to do it. Whether you take him up on it is up to you.

What is the relief meter?

The relief meter (出氣值) at the top of the screen rises with how often you hit and how hard each item hits, and the villain looks more battered as it goes up. Attack quickly and a combo count (連打) appears at the top right: any item counts as long as the hits come close together, a longer combo fills the meter faster, and it resets when you pause. You can send him off after the first hit; you don’t need to fill the meter.

What happens when I send him off?

Tap 送走小人 (Send him off) or drag him into the brazier, and he burns up in the brazier. A small slip of paper rises with a little stamp showing how many you’ve sent off this year, and you can choose 再打一個 (Hit another) or 完成 (Done). The villain hitting tab shows how many villains you’ve sent off this year; that’s the only villain hitting data the app keeps.

Calendar, reminders and widgets

What’s on the calendar tab?
  • At the top, a greeting that changes with the time of day.
  • A calendar page: the date and day of the week, the lunar date, the year’s stem-branch name and zodiac animal, and today’s solar term, festivals, deity birthdays, the 1st and 15th of the lunar month, and Zuoya (做牙, the merchants’ offering days). Tap the solar term, a festival, or the 1st/15th row to read about it; tap a deity’s birthday to go straight to that deity’s moon block page.
  • At the bottom, the daily quote: a title, a quote, and a short explanation.

Can I change the daily quote myself?

There’s no button to change it manually. The quote changes automatically each time you open or return to the app; on a new day, you see that day’s quote first.

Why does the greeting change?

It follows the time on your phone: 早安 (Good morning) from 5 a.m., 午安 (Good afternoon) from 11 a.m., 晚安 (Good evening) from 6 p.m., and 夜深了 (It’s late) from 11 p.m. until 5 a.m. The line underneath rotates daily. If you’ve entered a name in your personal details, the greeting includes it (unless it’s too long to fit).

How are the lunar calendar and solar terms worked out? Do they need the internet?

The lunar calendar, solar terms and festivals are all calculated on your phone, with no internet needed. Lunar dates follow Taiwan time.

Where do the almanac’s auspicious and inauspicious activities come from?

They follow the Xieji Bianfang Shu (協紀辨方書), the source most Taiwanese almanacs use, worked out on your phone from the solar-term month and the day’s stem-branch, with no internet needed. Almanacs from different publishers differ a little, so treat it as a reference. Medical items (求醫, 治病, 針灸) are left out: if you feel unwell, see a doctor as usual. The lookup table is ported from the open-source library lunar-javascript (MIT License); the full license is at the bottom of 設定 › 資料與隱私 › 免責說明 (Settings › Data & privacy › Disclaimer).

How do I turn reminders on and off?

The first time you reach the main screen, the temple keeper asks whether you’d like a daily reminder. Tap 好，提醒我 (Yes, remind me) and allow notifications to turn them on, or tap 先不用 (Not now) and you won’t be asked again.

You can change them at any time in 設定 › 聲音與提醒 (Settings › Sound & reminders):

  • 每日佳句 (Daily quote): one every morning at 7 a.m.
  • 節氣提醒 (Solar term reminder): a reminder on the day of each solar term.
  • 拜拜的日子 (Worship days): deity birthdays, Tianshe Day, the 1st and 15th, festivals and Zuoya, each with its own switch; for deity birthdays you can pick which deities to be reminded of.

If you’ve turned them on but nothing arrives, check that notifications are allowed in your iPhone’s Settings › Notifications › 問筊. Reminders are scheduled by the phone itself and don’t need the internet.

What time do reminders arrive?

Morning reminders always arrive at 7 a.m.; the time can’t be changed. If several things fall on the same day (say, the daily quote, a solar term and the 1st of the lunar month), they are combined into one notification whose title says what the day is, such as 今天是小雪 or 今天是媽祖聖誕. Deity birthdays, Tianshe Day and festivals also get a reminder at 8 p.m. the evening before; the 1st and 15th and Zuoya are only reminded on the morning of the day.

How do I add widgets to the Home Screen or Lock Screen?

There are two widgets:

  • 今日 (Today): today’s lunar date, solar term and daily quote. It comes in small and medium sizes for the Home Screen and can also go on the Lock Screen.
  • 擲筊 (Moon blocks): tap it to throw once. You don’t choose a deity, and nothing is recorded. It comes in small and medium sizes for the Home Screen.

To add one to the Home Screen:

  1. Touch and hold an empty spot on the Home Screen until the icons start to jiggle.
  2. Tap Edit at the top left, then Add Widget.
  3. Search for 問筊, swipe to the size you like, and tap Add Widget.

To add one to the Lock Screen:

  1. Touch and hold the Lock Screen, tap Customize, then choose Lock Screen.
  2. Tap the widget area below the time and choose 問筊.

In the app, 設定 › 桌面小工具 (Settings › Widgets) shows the same steps, with a preview of each widget.

How long does a result stay on the moon block widget?

Tap it to throw once, without opening the app or choosing a deity; the throw is not written to the app’s history. The result stays on the widget for a while, then the blocks gather themselves back side by side. When you throw several shengbei in a row, the widget shows how many in a row; the count starts over once the blocks are gathered back. Widgets have no sound or vibration.

Why is the quote on the widget different from the one in the app?

The 今日 (Today) widget shows that day’s quote and changes at midnight, while the daily quote in the app changes every time you open it, so the two can differ. Until you’ve opened the app once, the widget shows a note about the solar term or festival instead.

Sound and display

There’s no sound.

Sound follows your iPhone’s silent mode, so first make sure your phone isn’t on silent. Then check that 音效 (Sound effects) and 背景聲 (Background sound) are on in 設定 › 聲音與提醒 (Settings › Sound & reminders). Vibration is 觸覺回饋 (Haptics) on the same screen.

What is the background sound? Will it stop the music I’m listening to?

The background sound is gentle music that plays throughout the app: the first time you open it, it starts on the welcome screen; after that, it starts when you reach the main screen and keeps playing as you switch tabs. During meditation it changes to the sound of a singing bowl, and the villain hitting tab has a livelier tune of its own. You can turn off just 背景聲 (Background sound) in 設定 › 聲音與提醒 (Settings › Sound & reminders) and keep the sound effects.

The app’s sounds don’t stop music playing in other apps; they play alongside it. When your phone is on silent, the app stays silent too.

How do I switch to dark mode or make the text bigger?

Go to 設定 › 畫面 (Settings › Display):

  • 外觀 (Appearance): 跟隨系統 (Match system), 明亮 (Light) or 深色 (Dark).
  • 字體大小 (Text size): 跟隨系統 (Match system), 標準 (Standard), 大 (Large) or 特大 (Extra large).
  • Turn off 顯示提示 (Show hints) to hide the small help buttons on each screen.

What changes when Reduce Motion is on?

When Reduce Motion is on in your iPhone’s Settings › Accessibility › Motion, Wenjiao tones down its animations:

  • Moon blocks: the blocks don’t fly; you see them where they land.
  • Opening: the blocks’ flight isn’t shown.
  • Poem lots: the full-screen animation is replaced by a simple cylinder-shaking animation.
  • Villain hitting: the screen doesn’t shake.

Results are the same as usual; you just skip the motion.

Records and data

Where are my moon block and poem lot records?

In 設定 › 請示紀錄 (Settings › History). You can filter by deity, way of asking or result, and view statistics. Open an entry to edit the question you noted, add what happened later, or share a card.

Every throw and draw is recorded. If you’d rather not keep one, you can delete it; see the next question.

How do I delete records or clear data?
  • Delete one entry: in 設定 › 請示紀錄 (Settings › History), swipe the entry left and tap 刪除 (Delete), or open it and tap 刪除這筆紀錄 (Delete this record).
  • Delete all moon block and poem lot records: 設定 › 資料與隱私 › 清除所有紀錄 (Settings › Data & privacy › Clear all records).
  • Clear personal details: tap the name card at the top of 設定 (Settings), then 清除個人資料 (Clear personal details).
  • Worship data (devotion points, practice and rank, streak, offerings collection and worship records) has no separate clear button; to clear it, delete the app.
  • Deleting the app deletes all of its data on this phone, and it can’t be restored.

If I delete the app or change phones, will my data still be there?

Your data is stored only on this iPhone. The app has no cloud sync and we don’t hold any of your data, so we can’t recover it for you. If you delete the app, its data is deleted with it. Whether your data moves to a new phone depends on your iPhone’s own backup and transfer.

Is my data included in iCloud Backup?

Wenjiao has no cloud sync of its own and never sends your data to us. If your iPhone uses iCloud Backup (or a computer backup), the app’s data may be part of that iPhone backup, which Apple handles according to your system settings; if you later restore your iPhone from that backup, your records may come back with it. Deleting the app and downloading it again on its own won’t bring the data back.

Do you collect my data?

No. The app needs no account and has no ads or analytics tools. Your records and personal details are stored only on your iPhone, and we can’t see them. The full details are in our Privacy Policy, and you’ll also find it in the app under 設定 › 資料與隱私 › 隱私權政策 (Settings › Data & privacy › Privacy policy).

資料與隱私 (Data & privacy) shows a warning that the records file can’t be opened.

This means the app couldn’t read its records file on your phone this time and is using temporary records instead, which won’t be kept after you close the app. Close the app completely and open it again; if the warning keeps appearing, please write to us.

Troubleshooting

My reminders aren’t arriving.

Check these in order:

  1. The reminder you want is turned on in 設定 › 聲音與提醒 (Settings › Sound & reminders).
  2. Notifications are allowed in your iPhone’s Settings › Notifications › 問筊.
  3. If a Focus is on, reminders may go quietly to Notification Center instead of appearing or making a sound.
  4. If you haven’t opened the app in a long time: reminders are scheduled on the phone about a month ahead and rescheduled the first time you open the app each day. If you haven’t opened it for more than a month, open it once and they’ll start again.

There’s no vibration.

Check that 觸覺回饋 (Haptics) is on in 設定 › 聲音與提醒 (Settings › Sound & reminders). Widgets themselves can’t vibrate; that’s a system limitation.

A widget isn’t updating or looks wrong.

The 今日 (Today) widget changes to a new page at midnight by itself, without the internet. If the date hasn’t changed or no quote appears:

  1. Open Wenjiao once. The widget reads settings saved by the app to work out your daily quote.
  2. If it still looks wrong, remove the widget from your Home Screen and add it again.

The text is too small or too large.

Change it in 設定 › 畫面 › 字體大小 (Settings › Display › Text size). With 跟隨系統 (Match system), the app follows your iPhone’s text size (Settings › Display & Brightness › Text Size); 標準 (Standard), 大 (Large) and 特大 (Extra large) stay at that size whatever your phone is set to. At very large sizes, some screens stack their content vertically so text isn’t cut off.

The typefaces look different from the screenshots.

The Kai (楷體) and Song (宋體) typefaces on screen are system fonts that iOS downloads from Apple. Until the download finishes, or when you’re offline, the app uses the standard system font instead; once you’re online, the system downloads them in the background and they’ll appear after that.

Membership and supporting the team

What is “Support the team”?

There’s a box at the back right of the altar table in the worship tab. Tap it to open 支持開發團隊 (Support the team), where you can choose one of three amounts: a cup of tea (一杯茶), a cup of coffee (一杯咖啡) or a snack (一份點心). The price shown on screen is what you pay.

This supports the team that makes the app; it isn’t a donation to a temple or any organization. Paying is optional, and every feature works the same whether or not you pay.

What do I get for paying?

Nothing in exchange: no devotion points, no rank, and no effect on the results of moon blocks or poem lots. Once it’s done, the screen simply says 謝謝你的支持 (Thank you for your support). You can support with the same amount more than once.

How do I know whether my payment went through?

When it’s done, the screen says 謝謝你的支持 (Thank you for your support). If you see 這次沒有完成，晚一點再試。 (This didn’t go through. Please try again later.), the purchase wasn’t completed. If you tap Cancel on the payment screen, you aren’t charged and the app shows no message. The app doesn’t keep a record of what you’ve bought; your Apple Account has the actual payment record.

Do I need to restore purchases after changing phones or reinstalling?

Not for supporting the team, since it doesn’t give you anything in return. If you’re a member, after changing phones or reinstalling, go to 設定 › 會員 (Settings › Membership) and tap 恢復購買 (Restore Purchases). Your membership comes back as long as you use the same Apple Account.

How do I cancel my membership?

Cancel in your iPhone’s Settings › Apple Account › Subscriptions, or in the app under 設定 › 會員 › 管理訂閱 (Settings › Membership › Manage subscription). After you cancel, you can keep using it until the end of the current period. If you cancel during the free trial, you won’t be charged.

How do I get a refund?

Payments are handled by Apple. We can’t see your payment details and can’t issue refunds ourselves. Go to Apple’s Report a Problem page at reportaproblem.apple.com, sign in with your Apple Account, choose to request a refund, and follow the steps on screen. Apple decides whether to grant the refund.

The screen says it can’t connect to the App Store, or that a purchase is waiting for approval.
  • 現在連不上 App Store，晚一點再試。 (Can’t connect to the App Store right now. Please try again later.): make sure your phone is online, wait a moment, and tap 再試一次 (Try again).
  • 這一筆在等待核准，核准之後會自動完成。 (This purchase is waiting for approval and will complete automatically once approved.): this usually means Ask to Buy is on in Family Sharing. Once a parent approves it, the purchase completes automatically; you don’t need to tap again.

About results

Are the results accurate?

Moon block and poem lot results are decided by a random number generator on your phone. The 3D animation only acts out the result; it doesn’t mean a deity is actually answering.

On every throw, each block has an even chance of landing flat side up, so shengbei comes up about half the time, and xiaobei and yinbei each about a quarter of the time; libei comes up about once in a thousand throws. When drawing lots, every lot has the same chance. Whether you pay, what you offer and how high your rank is never change these odds.
